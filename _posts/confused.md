I am confused about many things, but one thing I am certain of is that I need my body to change. I feel a deep sense of dysphoria over not having breasts and the inability to wear women’s clothing the way I’d like, the off-the-shoulder, bandeau, tube style dresses, etc. My facial hair is a constant source of irritation. I remember as a child, I would insist that my mother and grandmother put turmeric on my face to prevent hair growth; an effort that may or may not have worked, as my beard growth is quite sparse today.

I chose the name Aparna back in 2014, long before I fully realized I was trans-feminine. I don't mind what people call me, but a female-presenting person with feminine features wearing traditional "male" clothes is a bit unconventional, isn't it?

I recently updated my name at work to Aparna. Even though people still use my deadname, it doesn't really bother me. I know that I am a trans woman, yet I find myself selecting "non-binary" whenever possible. I’m still navigating whether that’s okay, especially since I don't particularly mind which pronouns people use; he, she, or they are all fine by me.

I have a feeling that once my physical features begin to shift, I might actually wear feminine clothes less often. Right now, I feel like I’m "performing" to gain external validation, but once I possess the features I currently lack, I won't have to work so hard to convince myself of who I am.

My wardrobe is now a collection of dresses, skirts, tights, leggings, and lingerie. I’ve kept about 30 T-shirts, five shirts and 2 jeans, which I’m sure will come in handy for "boymodding." Interestingly, I know my women's clothing sizes much better than my men's sizes now. It makes sense; until I was 18, my clothes were always chosen by family or friends. Now, the choice is finally mine.

Every morning, I wake up with the same intrusive thought: What if I can’t find enough Estrogen one day? The idea of losing access to it sends me into a panic.

I also find myself constantly pondering a question my mother asked: "How do you plan to hide the physical changes at your brother’s wedding in November?" By then, I will be seven months into HRT. My body will look different. What will I say to my father, who still knows nothing? What if I’m expected to participate in a ritual that requires me to be shirtless?

And while I despise my facial hair and shave it daily, I know I will have to make the sacrifice and let it grow out when I visit India.

Regardless of what happens, I will be in the UK for at least two more years. I am going to proceed with HRT. There is nothing more painful than seeing women on the street possessing the very traits I lack and wearing the very clothes I desire.
