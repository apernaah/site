# Realising who I am

It’s been almost a year since I came out. Looking back, this past year has been a period of profound realisation, exploration, and transition. From visiting Birmingham to meet Riya twice and India, to traveling to Argentina as myself where I came out to the Debian community and proudly wore a saree to the conference dinner... so much has shifted. I have plenty of stories from these trips, but I'll save those for separate posts.

Instead of just exploring new places, I spent this time exploring myself: buying clothes, experimenting with makeup, and navigating what it means to live as myself. I like to break this journey down into three distinct phases:

- Phase 1 (November 2025 - January 2026): My baseline. I started shaving my face leaving out the shadow, wearing clothes I actually felt comfortable in, and coming out to my close friends, cousins, mom, brother, roommates, and friends in the UK.

- Phase 2 (February 2026 - May 2026): I expanded my routine, testing out makeup products, and came out at work as well as to members of the tech community.

- Phase 3 (June 2026 - Present): I started Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT) in May 2026, which has gradually shaped my face, upper body, and lower body. I finally nailed down my daily makeup look - a simple concealer, eyeliner, mascara and a crimson lipstick that I get compliments on almost every day, and I love it. It was also during this phase that I made my trip to Argentina.

Despite all this progress, navigating daily life still brings moments of awkwardness. Walking around my house and neighborhood can feel exposing, and from an outside perspective, someone might look at me and think I haven't fully accepted myself as a trans woman.

The truth is, I know I am a woman. But being a recovering people pleaser, it's hard not to hyperfixate on sideways glances or worry about what random people are thinking. What intimidates me the most are other South-Indian women, specifically seeing random Malayalee girls in public spaces. It triggers an immediate panic, especially in places like restaurants or movie theaters where I'm stuck for a while. For instance, when I went to watch Khalifa in cinemas, two girls sitting nearby kept turning around, staring, pointing, and whispering. I knew it was about me because I was the only person sitting in that row and corner.

It makes me wonder: why can't people just mind their own business and let others live authentically? When people migrate to different countries, naturalise, and become citizens, society accepts it. So why is it so hard to accept someone simply living as who they are, especially when I'm not harming anyone?

Another logistical hurdle has been managing my legal identity versus my preferred name. Thanks to Monzo, I finally have a debit card with my preferred name, and I’ve managed to update almost all my UK accounts to Aparna, though my passport, visa, and a few legacy bank accounts still need updating.

On the social side, my dating life is a bit of an on/off experiment. I'm attracted to both men and women, but my non-negotiable is that both genders must see me as a woman. Interestingly, I find myself becoming more feminine when talking to men who are attracted to me, yet I vastly prefer hanging out with women. It sounds a bit contradictory, but it's my reality.

Ultimately, my goals right now are simple: I want to live peacefully, pay off my education loan, land a solid job, and by my late thirties, become a mother to a daughter and give her everything I possibly can.
